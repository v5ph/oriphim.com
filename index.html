The Distinction

Verification and validation are not the same claim.

Verification

Was the mathematics solved correctly? Numerical error budgets, convergence under refinement, conservation, integrator cross-agreement, dimensional consistency, and sensitivity of the conclusion to every free parameter. This tier needs no reference data. It is available for any system you can describe. Most published computational work has never received it.

Validation

How far is the model from reality? Discrepancy against measurement, with uncertainty carried on both sides — not one representative run compared to an experimental mean and called reasonable. This tier needs reality to compare against: a published benchmark, an inter-laboratory result, or your own test campaign.

Oriphim says which tier a report belongs to, on the report. It does not produce a report it cannot stand behind.

The Deliverable

A package, in the form the review board already reads.

Objectives and margin summary. Load cases and boundary conditions. How the components were modelled and meshed. Solver checks — mesh quality, ground checks, force balance. Margins per part with the critical case shown. Hand calcs. Fatigue and fracture where the program requires it. Bolted joints.

Hours instead of months. A named person reviews it, corrects it, and signs it. Oriphim captures every correction.

Judgment stays with the engineer. Where a credibility framework requires a practitioner's signature, that requirement is the product specification, not a constraint on it.

Limits

Oriphim does not claim a model is correct.

At the verification tier it claims the model was solved to a stated accuracy, and that the conclusions are or are not stable under stated perturbations. At the validation tier it claims the model is wrong by a quantified amount under stated conditions.

Both are defensible. Neither is “good agreement.”

No number is written by a language model. A model may propose a brief and write interpretive prose. Every number in a report comes from deterministic computation.

Scope

Bounded by what you can describe.

Verification is available for any system you can pose — a warped accretion disc has no mesh, no machinery and no CAD model, and Oriphim handles it natively. Validation is earned one domain at a time, and Oriphim states the limit explicitly wherever reality is not available to compare against.

FEA is one solver backend. CAD is one geometry input. Both matter when a system has geometry and are irrelevant when it doesn't.
