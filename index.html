How it works

One claim, start to finish. Nothing runs until you sign the brief.

  1. RebuildOri reads the equations, parameters and assumptions out of the paper and ties each one to the page it came from.
  2. SignYou review the brief: the goal, the numbers, and how close counts as a match. One signature, then it runs.
  3. RunThe calculation runs as plain code, with the domain's own convergence and conservation checks.
  4. CompareEach result sits beside the printed number, inside or outside the band agreed before the run.
Limits

Trust the numbers, not the abstract.

Oriphim never tells you a paper is right. A match means two calculations agree, not that the world does. The report says what was measured, how accurate the computation was, and how far that lands from the printed result.

A language model drafts the brief and the prose. Every number comes from code that ran, and every model call is kept in the folder.

Holds
The rerun lands inside the band agreed before the run.
Doesn't hold
It lands outside, and the report says by how much.
Can't tell
The paper leaves out something a rerun needs, and the report names it.
