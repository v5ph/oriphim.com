Why is the first Claim Check free?

Because the fastest way to see what Ori does is to watch it work on your own problem. I take on a small number of free checks for funded teams with a computational claim Ori can run. When it’s delivered, we talk about whether a licence makes sense.

What does the stamp mean?

A stamped run bundle records exactly which checks Ori ran and how each one came out: convergence under refinement, conservation, agreement between integrators, sensitivity to free parameters, and comparison with any reported results. It also lists what was not assessed, and anyone with the bundle can rerun it and get the same numbers.

It is not my endorsement of your conclusions, and it never says a model is correct. That is verification: whether the model solves its own equations correctly. Validation, whether the model matches reality, needs your data and your judgment. The judgment stays with your team.

What does support cover?

Support means Ori itself: installing it, using it, reading its output, and fixing anything that breaks. It doesn’t cover me checking your models, joining your reviews, or signing off on your results. If you want an independent check on another claim, ask me.

How is a licence priced?

Per seat, sold in blocks of 5, and each block costs less than the one before, so a larger team pays less per seat. Every seat gets the whole product. Tell me how many people will use Ori and I’ll send a quote, usually within two business days.

A physicist in the US earns a median of about $172,000 a year. Wages are about 70% of what an employer spends on a person, so that comes to roughly $20,000 a month, and hiring one usually takes two months or more. Ori doesn’t replace that person. It lets the people you already have rebuild, run and stress-test a model in days rather than weeks, and hand reviewers a bundle they can rerun themselves.

The decisions these models feed, a go/no-go, a funding round, a partnership, usually run into the millions. The question is what one unchecked model would cost you.
